If you're here, something specific probably set you off. Maybe the energy limits on free accounts, or the AI features nobody asked for, or the pricing creeping up again.

Here's the thing though. That's usually not why people actually leave.

The real reason tends to show up much earlier. Around day 200 of a streak you're quite proud of, when you realise you still can't order lunch. The lessons weren't bad. They just didn't stick.

Worth sorting that out before you install anything, because most alternatives fix the opposite problem. They hand you a better drill. If structure was what you were missing, great. If it was a reason to care, a more serious course makes things worse, not better.

## Figure out what you're actually missing

Four things usually go wrong, and they point at completely different apps.

You might never have learned *why* anything works. You can pick the right tile without knowing what rule you just used. That's a course problem.

Or you read fine and freeze the second someone talks to you. Speaking problem, and it's the one Duolingo is worst at.

Or the words evaporate. You learn something, meet it again three weeks later, gone.

Or your language was one of the thin courses and you ran out of anything good.

## If you need structure

**Babbel** is the obvious answer and usually the right one. Built as a course, grammar explained rather than implied, and it goes somewhere. Fewer languages than Duolingo, and noticeably less fun, which is a real cost if the streak was doing the heavy lifting for you.

**Busuu** works similarly but adds something no algorithm has replaced yet. Native speakers correct your writing and speaking, and you correct theirs. Quality varies, since these are volunteers rather than teachers. When it's good it's very good.

**LingoDeer** was built for Japanese, Korean and Chinese and it shows. If your language works nothing like English, LingoDeer explains the structure instead of expecting you to absorb it by osmosis. Handles European languages fine, but that's not the reason to pick it.

## If you need to speak

Duolingo's biggest genuine hole, and there's a whole category of apps that exist because of it. Some pair you with AI, some with actual humans doing an exchange.

Choose based on how much you want to squirm. AI is patient, available at 2am, and never judges your accent. Humans are better teachers and considerably more frightening.

## If nothing stays in your head

**Memrise** leans on repeated exposure plus clips of native speakers using words in real speech rather than studio audio. Good if you need volume.

**Drops** is five minutes a day, vocabulary only, and honest about being vocabulary only. Great second app, poor only app.

## If your language is barely supported

**Ling** covers sixty plus languages, including plenty everyone else ignores. Depth varies a lot and the small courses are thin, but for some languages it's the only serious option going.

**Mondly** is wide, visual, bite sized, and periodically sells lifetime access instead of a subscription, which suits people who hate monthly billing. Check what's on offer before assuming, since that changes.

## What I'd actually do

If you finished a course and still can't hold a conversation, go to Babbel or Busuu and accept it'll feel more like studying. That's the trade.

If you left because you were bored, don't reach for the most serious app on the list. You'll quit that too, just faster. Pair something structured with something you'll actually open on a Tuesday night.

And if you didn't really want to leave at all, don't. Duolingo is very good at getting people to show up every day, which is the part almost everyone fails at. Keep the streak. Add a second app for the bit it doesn't do.
